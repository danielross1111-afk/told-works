The Practice

He builds the feeling first.

For more than twenty-five years, Dan has built at the demanding end of New Zealand residential architecture. He no longer builds houses. Now he works alone, on a small number of commissions each year, for the people and places worth the time it takes.

Every space — a house, a room, a stretch of water — has a story it is reaching for. Sometimes the story is set down in an architect's drawings. Sometimes it lives only as a feeling in the person who will move through the room. Dan listens for it. What follows is made in answer.

The form changes. The intent does not: to give the place the thing it was already asking for. A room finished so completely it falls quiet.

Three to four commissions a year. He visits the site before he agrees to anything.

"Timber passes through many hands. By the time it reaches us, it has already lived a long life. You have to honour that tree — give it justice, let its beauty be seen.

When timber, stone and concrete are brought together with care, you reunite elements that once lived together in nature. Done well, they resonate as one."

— Dan Ross

N° 02 · Legacy The Sixteen — installed
Private legacy commission · Lake Hayes

The Sixteen.

Sixteen ironbark figures reclaimed from a roadside ditch — 300-year-old Western Australian timber. Stripped to heartwood, refined by fire, crowned with hand-carved serpentine and Aotea stone. Set back into the ground as permanent guardians.

N° 03 The Threshold — completed
Commission · Entrance gate

The Threshold.

The original gates were expensive, automated, and wrong — they announced when they should have concealed. Re-clad over the existing structure with aged timber palings at varied heights. The mountain and lake are now revealed only once you pass through.

The Standard

Reputation observed,
not claimed.

The work does not lie about the person who made it.

There is a pattern across Dan's career that no single project illustrates — only the full body of work makes it visible.

When projects have hit pressure — and they have — Dan has not walked away, renegotiated downward, or settled for less. He has absorbed the cost, found the material, and finished what he committed to finish.

It runs the other way too. He chooses the people he works for as carefully as he chooses the timber he works with. Three to four commissions a year. The first conversation is a site visit. If the brief, the place and the person are not aligned, the conversation ends there, with respect and without obligation.

Twenty-five years of those choices, made consistently, is not a portfolio. It is a practice.

  • Every current commission sourced through direct referral or firsthand experience.
  • Zero advertising in twenty-five years.
  • Repeat clients across multiple commissions spanning years.
  • Standard maintained through budget pressure, scope change and difficult timelines.
Commission

Begin a conversation.

Each commission begins in conversation, and not every conversation becomes a piece. Dan takes on the work he believes he is the right hand for — when the place, the people, and the brief are aligned, the rest follows.

  1. i.
    A first email
    A note to Dan describing the place, the people, and what the work needs to do. As detailed or as brief as suits.
  2. ii.
    A site visit
    Dan visits in person. He listens to the place. No commitment in either direction at this point.
  3. iii.
    A clear answer
    A yes or a no within a week of the visit, and either way, an honest read on the brief.
