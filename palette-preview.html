Palette study

Four directions, each tuned against the references you sent — moodier greens, deeper coppers, warmer bones. The current palette sits at the bottom for direct comparison.

Each option is rendered against the actual UI patterns from the site so we can judge them in situ rather than as abstract swatches.

My read Of the four, N° 01 — Pounamu Twilight is the one I'd push you toward. It keeps the cultural anchor of pounamu (the green is still there, still meaningful), but pulls it from the slightly clean "kiwiana" brightness into a deeper bush-at-dusk forest tone — the green you actually see at the edge of native bush after sundown. Paired with a true burnt copper in place of the current tan ember, it reads exactly like the references: rich, restrained, naturally luxurious. N° 02 — Schist & Tussock is the boldest move (drops green entirely for Otago's autumn-grass and stone palette) and is the most locally truthful to Queenstown specifically. N° 03 — Lake & Ember is the most editorial / magazine-like. N° 04 — Full Moon Night is moody but probably too dusty-rose and away from the "made by hand from raw materials" feel.
N° 01 Pounamu Twilight Recommended

The current spirit, deepened. Pounamu drops from leaf-green to bush-at-dusk forest. Ember becomes a true burnt copper. Bone warms a touch. Reads as: the bush at last light, fire in the workshop.

N° 02 Schist & Tussock Most local

Drops green entirely. Leans into the dry-grass / schist-stone / autumn-copper palette of Central Otago — what's actually outside the workshop window. Boldest move; most place-specific. Some risk it reads more "earth" than "luxury" without careful typography.

N° 03 Lake & Ember Most editorial

Closest to the deep-teal & copper inspirations (palettes 2 & 3 of your set). Pulls the green toward water rather than bush — Wakatipu under cloud. A small step away from "pounamu" semantically, but the most directly luxurious / magazine-like of the four.
